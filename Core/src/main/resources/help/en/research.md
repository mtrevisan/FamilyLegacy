# Research

Family Legacy separates three things that are usually mixed:

- **assertions**: what a source says;
- **hypotheses**: possible interpretations, such as "these two records may be the same person";
- **conclusions**: what the researcher decides after weighing the evidence.

Because they are separate records, a wrong conclusion can be revised without losing the evidence it was drawn from. The **Research** menu holds the commands.

## Planning the research

- **Research Board...** shows the whole workflow as a tree: *question, activity, task*, with the conclusions of each question next to its activities. Select a node to see its details on the right; double-click it (or press *Edit*) to open its editor. With a question selected, **New** offers *Activity*, *Task* and *Conclusion of the selected question* with the question already filled in. The filter field keeps only the matching branches.
- **Research Questions...** lists the questions with the number of activities, tasks and conclusions that refer to each, and lets you create, edit and delete them.
- **Research Activities...** lists the activities with their question, type, status, action and result.
- **Research Tasks...** lists the tasks with their question, description, status, priority and due date.

The management lists are the tabular way to browse and delete the records of one type; the board is the way to see how they fit together.

## Weighing the evidence

- **Conclusions...** lists every conclusion with its issue, proof status, the number of records it resolves and its narrative.
- **Identity Hypotheses...** lists the hypotheses, each with the two candidates it compares and a comment. An identity hypothesis is how you record "these two individuals may be the same person" without merging them. A conclusion on the hypothesis then says whether it is proven or disproven.

A conclusion lists the records it resolves, states the **proof status** and explains the reasoning. The assertions it is about stay as they are.

## Evidence qualifiers

Records can carry *evidence qualifiers*, which say how good a piece of evidence is, along three dimensions:

- the **source type**: original or derived;
- the **information type**: primary, secondary or undetermined;
- the **evidence type**: direct, indirect or negative.

**Research > Evidence Qualifiers...** is a report on how they are distributed in the file: an overview of the records with and without qualifiers, and one tab for each dimension. The records with no qualifiers at all are the most common gap in a file that has not been fully assessed.

## Reporting

- **Proof Summary...** shows what has been concluded and how solidly. One tab counts the conclusions for each proof status (*conflicting evidence*, *supported*, *proven*, *disproven*; conclusions with another status are grouped apart, so none is dropped); the other lists them and can be filtered by status and opened with a double-click.
- **Research Log...** is a read-only, chronological view of every research activity, ordered by creation date. Activities without a date are placed at the end.
- **Reports > Research Progress...** produces a report of the state of the research. See [Reports](reports.md).

## Getting help from the assistant

The [Research Assistant](assistant.md) notices open problems and can create drafts of the records that deal with them: a research question for a gap, a conclusion for contradicting evidence, an identity hypothesis for two records that look alike.
